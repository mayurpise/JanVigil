Reporting guide

How to report a bribe

  1. Don’t pay the bribe

    If a public servant asks for money to do their official duty, don’t pay, and don’t agree to pay yet.

  2. Call the anti-corruption bureau before any money changes hands

    Use your state’s line (1064 in many states, 14400 in Andhra Pradesh, 1031 in Delhi; check your state’s page), or go to the nearest bureau office.

  3. The bureau sets up the trap with you

    Officers record your complaint, bring in independent witnesses (panchas), and dust the currency notes with phenolphthalein powder so the hand-over leaves proof.

  4. The official is caught taking the marked notes

    Officers arrest the official on the spot, and a case is registered under the Prevention of Corruption Act.

  1. State anti-corruption bureau or vigilance directorate

    The first stop for bribe traps, disproportionate assets and official misconduct.

  2. State Lokayukta or the Lokpal

    The statutory ombudsman for corruption and maladministration by public functionaries, including ministers and MLAs.

  3. CPGRAMS, the Central Vigilance Commission or the CBI

    For complaints about central government ministries, public sector undertakings, nationalised banks and union territory administrations.
